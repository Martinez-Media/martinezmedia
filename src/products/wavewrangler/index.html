Get the timing right.
Then make it yours.

Recorders start late. Timing drifts. A good conversation deserves more than a guess.

Align Tracks proposes timing for you to review, never silently accepting it. Use manual anchors and timing adjustments, or adaptive timing for changing delay, then listen before proceeding.

Adaptive timing distinguishes supported matches from estimated gaps and ends. Keeping audio is not the same as verifying every moment.

How alignment works

From first listen to final mix.

Listen. Review. Edit.

Local Whisper transcripts help you find words and review fillers. Adjust the boundaries before making synchronized cuts or muting just one speaker. Transcription and word timing are approximate.

Give the episode its shape.

Add music and other Audio Clips. Trim, split, fade, and shape levels. Ordinary placement overlays the timeline; an intro or time insertion is a separate, previewed action.

Mix for the finish.

Balance per-speaker input gain, faders, and pan. Add Audio Unit inserts and Master processing. Listen in Production Mix mode, then export WAV, MP3, or M4A/AAC-LC with optional loudness normalization.

The edit lives here.
Your originals stay yours.

Local-first isn’t an upload button in disguise.

Original recordings are referenced in place, not rewritten. Editing decisions live in your show document, with Undo and Redo.

Whisper inference runs on your Mac. Model downloads happen when you request them; recordings and transcripts are not uploaded for transcription.

A verified local save does not mean a cloud provider has finished syncing your files. Recovery offers and save failures stay visible.

A deliberate workflow.
A finished episode.

  1. Create a show and episode in Library.
  2. Import recordings and assign each speaker’s Primary.
  3. Align, review, and explicitly accept timing.
  4. Transcribe locally, listen, and make your edits.
  5. Add Audio Clips and balance the Production Mix.
  6. Review the episode range, metadata, and export.

Where things stand

WaveWrangler is under active development. There are no published GitHub releases. Building from source is the current way to try it.

The development target is macOS 26 or later on Apple silicon, using Xcode with the macOS 26 SDK and Swift 6. This is not a claim of qualification across every Mac, format, or Audio Unit.

App Store distribution is planned. There is no release date, download, or commercial availability promise.
